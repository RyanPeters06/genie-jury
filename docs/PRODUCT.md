# Product brief — Genie Jury

## The idea

Hackathon builders often ask a chatbot whether an idea is good and receive a flattering answer. Genie Jury is the more useful opposite: a theatrical live pitch where four distinct AI jurors interrupt only after the builder finishes, pressure-test real assumptions, and leave the builder with the smallest credible next move.

The target user is a solo hackathon builder or early product founder who needs to decide whether an idea is worth building before demo day. The primary success case is a 90-second demo that feels like a game but produces one concrete scope cut, one evidence-backed question, and one user-focused test.

## Experience

1. **Enter the sky.** The builder sees Ember, Gale, Tide, and Volt seated on clouds in a bright daytime sky and chooses live voice or typed fallback.
2. **Pitch uninterrupted.** The builder speaks or types their case. The stage remains art-led: no dashboard, transcript wall, or fake research panel.
3. **Deliberate honestly.** Gale extracts claims and researches the highest-risk one. Ember, Tide, and Volt each challenge a different angle.
4. **Recover.** The Ally converts the pressure into a narrow, buildable plan and the four jurors leave simple votes.

## Evidence standard

- **Verified:** an inspectable source supports the claim.
- **Contested:** credible sources disagree or the signal is inconclusive.
- **Unproven:** the claim needs a user test or further research.

The jury may be funny, but evidence language must never pretend a source exists when it does not.

## Visual direction

The supplied Genie Jury sprite sheet is canonical. Every juror sits forward on a cloud with crossed arms. The top row is listening; the bottom row is speaking. The stage uses soft daytime blue, clouds, sun bloom, and sparse speaker cues. The active juror rises and gains emphasis while the others recede.
